Method: first check whether each item substantively reports several independent occurrences. If one of them is a roundup that includes the other, judge ROUNDUP first; don't pick one product or speaker the two items both mention to stand for the whole item, and don't treat a conference wrap-up or conference summary as a follow-up to one of its products or talks. Then state each report's specific occurrence in one sentence (who, did what, to what, when) and compare them pair by pair. Exact publication times are given in UTC; one marked "原文仅有日期" (the original has only a date) keeps the source's calendar date and its time of day is unknown; a historical record marked "原始精度未知" (original precision unknown) can't be treated as an exact time. The same occurrence from sources abroad can fall on different calendar dates, so a date alone doesn't prove which came first; judge it together with the body. When unsure between SAME_OCCURRENCE and SAME_STORY, ask yourself: if both reports are true, did one thing happen in the world, or two directly related things one after the other? A product's lifecycle in itself is not one event.
