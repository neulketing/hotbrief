SAME_OCCURRENCE (the same occurrence): the same subject doing the same thing to the same specific object at the same time. Retellings across languages, different outlets' emphasis, more or fewer details, and an official launch video/blog/release notes don't change the identity. Several sizes, variants or base versions released together in one product line, and the features, pricing, availability plans and system card/technical report details of that launch, are the same occurrence; so are different reports of the same ruling, report, poll, incident, single interview or single talk, and repeated promotion of the same promotion/same deadline. The shared identity must land on a specific released object or a specific occurrence, not merely the same company, the same day, the same conference or the same umbrella announcement: separate product launches, different talks and different decisions within it are judged separately.

SAME_STORY (different developments of the same event): not the same occurrence, but in a direct sequence around the same specific occurrence: teaser/rumor and the official launch; a launch and its later listing, integration or free-access offer on a third-party platform; a launch and reviews, hands-on tests, leaderboard results, first impressions or analyses of it; an event and the parties' responses, official investigations, later disclosures or follow-up statements. Ongoing investigation or disclosure of the same exposed conduct may add affected parties; the list of parties need not be identical in every report, but the material must support the follow-up relationship. Two separate product updates, business results or feature launches don't become one event merely because they share a product, a release cycle or a time order.

UNRELATED (different things): two different launches of different products or versions (different dates or different publishers); two different things from the same company on the same day; different studies, different views or different incidents on the same topic; merely mentioning the same product or company. Tutorials, usage tips, personal opinions and marketing posts about a product are UNRELATED unless they clearly target that specific occurrence of the candidate (reviewing it, responding to it, listing it).

ROUNDUP: one side collects several independent occurrences and includes the other's event as one of several entries; this includes multi-topic roundups, daily and weekly digests, conference recaps, overviews of several independent products, and conference summaries of several speakers. A roundup and one single-event report within it can't be SAME_OCCURRENCE or SAME_STORY; a roundup mentioning two independent events also doesn't mean those two events can be merged. Using other events only as background or comparison is still judged by the core occurrence and doesn't automatically make it a roundup.
