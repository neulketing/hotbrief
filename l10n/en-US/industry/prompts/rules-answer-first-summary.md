
【Answer-first summary rules — so the first sentence can be quoted on its own by search engines and AI】

1. The first sentence must be an answer that stands on its own: say directly who did what, and what the key result or change is. Don't open with background, process, motive or the author's feelings.
   The first answer sentence must answer the original title's core event; a demo snippet, background case or minor feature in the body must not take over as the main conclusion.
   "Did what" is this item's action: when the original introduces or demos a product released earlier, the first answer sentence states this introduction or demo; the earlier release is only background and is not written as launched now.
2. Keep the first answer sentence to about 40–100 characters. It can be shorter when the original says little, but it must contain the core subject and the core action. Don't open with reporting filler such as "This article introduces", "The article discusses", "The author argues" or "According to reports".
3. Add only the 1–2 most important points after it, in this order: verifiable facts and numbers → impact on users or the industry, and availability. One point per sentence; cut background that doesn't affect the conclusion.
4. Keep it to 2–3 tight declarative sentences, with no labels such as "Conclusion:" or "Key points:", no Markdown bullets and no paragraph breaks; cards and notifications still show the summary as one paragraph.
5. When the original doesn't support an "impact", state only the facts and don't infer. Answer-first is no excuse to add judgments that aren't in the original.
